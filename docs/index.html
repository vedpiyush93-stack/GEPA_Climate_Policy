Can AI spot a climate target?

Governments write climate goals into laws and policies. Experts read passages from these documents and marked every target. A small AI model, coached with GEPA, read the same passages without ever seeing their country. Compare their answers, one passage at a time.

What counts as a target?

A goal with a number and a deadline. Each passage can hold any of three kinds, or none.

Net zero

A commitment to balance emissions with removals, reaching net zero.

Emission reduction

A promise to cut greenhouse gas emissions, nationwide or in one sector.

Other target

Any other goal with a number and a date, e.g. renewable energy.

How it works

1Expert labelsClimate Policy Radar's experts marked each passage: net zero target, emission reduction target, other target, or none.
2Unseen countriesCountries were split into five groups. Each group was held out once, so the AI never judged a country it had learned from.
3Coaching with GEPAA larger model, Claude Sonnet, read the small model's mistakes and rewrote its instructions, starting from the experts' own definition.
4Every passageThe coached small model, Claude Haiku, then labelled every passage of its unseen countries and explained each answer.
Passages, expert labels, countries and regions: Climate Policy Radar, national-climate-targets (CC BY 4.0; Juhasz et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.02822). Country names and flags are derived from the dataset's country codes. Method: GEPA (Agrawal et al., International Conference on Learning Representations 2026, arXiv:2507.19457). Code, data and cached model responses: GitHub.
